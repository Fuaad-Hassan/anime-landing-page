Attack on Titan

It’s hard to find a show that pulls you in as effectively as the first episode of Attack on Titan. The intense action is great, but the overarching mystery of where the Titans actually came from is what kept me completely invested.

Even if I had mixed feelings about a couple of narrative choices along the way, the execution of the twists was always phenomenal. The momentum and adrenaline of the series made it an unforgettable watch, always leaving you desperate for the next episode.


Action

Fate Stay Night

It’s hard not to talk about the animation first because it is genuinely jaw-dropping, which is exactly why I gave it a shot in the first place.

The fights are easily some of the best choreographed in the medium. When combine that visual quality with the unique heroic spirits and the intense clashes, it makes for an incredibly fun and fast-paced ride. 


Fantasy

Made in Abyss

What instantly draws me into Made in Abyss is the world-building. The Abyss a massive, uncharted chasm filled with bizarre ecosystems and ancient relics is one of the most fascinating settings in anime. As Riko and Reg descend further into it, the series perfectly balances a sense of grand adventure with creeping dread.

Every new layer introduces harsher environments and higher stakes. The contrast between the incredibly beautiful art style and the brutal, unforgiving nature of their journey keeps the tension high at all times. It is a brilliant piece of environmental storytelling.


Adventure

Naruto

If I had to pick a defining anime from my childhood, it would absolutely be Naruto. Back then, the draw was simple: the action, the underdog story, and the core themes of loyalty and never giving up.

But rewatching it as an adult completely changes the experience. You start to pick up on the political themes, the heavy backstories, and how intricately the world building ties together. It’s rare to find a nostalgic favorite that actually gets more layered when you revisit it years later.


Shounen

Vinland Saga

The historical Viking setting is an instant hook, offering a brutal and realistic backdrop right from the first episode. The action choreography is fantastic, but the real masterpiece here is the character writing.

The story follows Thorfinn, shifting from a boy blinded by a need for revenge to a young man desperately searching for a peaceful life. It’s a slow-burn transformation that turns a violent revenge story into a really profound look at redemption and personal growth.


Historical

Violet Evergarden

The first thing you'll notice is the animation it’s genuinely some of the best I've ever seen. But the story holds up just as well. About a former soldier named Violet who takes a job writing letters for people, which is a massive challenge for someone who spent her whole life on a battlefield and barely understands normal human interaction.

The pacing is great because it focuses on episodic, contained stories. It’s a highly polished, relaxing watch that balances striking visuals with solid character development.
